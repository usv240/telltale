What it does, in one line

TellTale stacks bursts from any fixed camera, removes the camera's own motion, measures ground creep to a hundredth of a pixel, forecasts the failure window, re-measures to verify it, and pages a person.

It uses classical OpenCV 5 computer vision (no neural network anywhere), the inverse-velocity method that mines have used for decades, and a small AWS footprint. A person approves every public alert.

Why this matters

Bingham Canyon, Utah

April 2013

Radar saw creep accelerate from 1 to 5 mm a day. The pit was evacuated that morning; 165 million tonnes came down that evening. No casualties.

Brienz, Switzerland

June 2023

86 residents evacuated on monitoring data. The rockslide stopped metres short of the village school.

Wayanad, India

July 2024

No deformation monitoring. About 400 people died.

The difference is an instrument that costs 100,000 to 500,000 USD per site. TellTale applies the same physics to a 30 to 50 USD camera, in daylight, for creep-type failures, and says clearly when it cannot see.

What the system does, step by step

  1. Capture. A camera photographs the slope in bursts of seven frames, a few times a day.
  2. Stack. Frames in a burst are registered and median-stacked. Heat shimmer averages toward zero; real movement does not.
  3. Stabilise. The operator marks ground that cannot move (bedrock, buildings off the slide). Features there give the camera's own motion, which is removed. If the "stable" ground still looks like it moved, the epoch is rejected as a CAMERA_EVENT. This single rule removes the largest class of false alarms.
  4. Measure. Thousands of small patches are matched between the reference and the new image with normalised cross-correlation, refined to a hundredth of a pixel. Patches without texture or with ambiguous matches are excluded, never guessed.
  5. Forecast. Displacement becomes velocity. When the short-term velocity stays above the long-term velocity, acceleration has begun. One over velocity then falls toward zero along a line; where it crosses the time axis is the forecast failure time. A bootstrap and a three-method ensemble give the window.
  6. Decide. QUIET, MOVING, ACCELERATING, CRITICAL. CRITICAL sends a message to the duty contact with approve and dismiss links. Nothing reaches the public without that decision.

What it cannot do

  • Brittle and liquefaction failures can fail without surface creep. TellTale will not warn of those.
  • Night, fog, heavy rain and a wet lens blind the camera. Such epochs are reported as BLIND or BLURRED and the forecast uncertainty widens. They are never shown as "no movement".
  • A camera standing on the moving ground produces CAMERA_EVENT verdicts on the days that matter. Mount it off the slide.
  • Vegetation decorrelates over seasons; coverage drops and the reference must be refreshed.

Questions people ask

Why not satellites?

Satellite radar revisits every few days to weeks, loses coherence on vegetation, and cannot follow the final hours to days of acceleration. A camera on site measures every few hours for the cost of a phone.

What camera do I need?

Any fixed camera with a stable mount and a view of the slope: an old phone, a Raspberry Pi camera, or existing CCTV. Longer focal lengths give finer resolution; a lens hood and heater keep it dry.

What do the time units mean?

Whatever the site uses: hours for a construction excavation, days for a natural slope, minutes for the tabletop demonstration. The physics is the same.

Who sees the alert?

The duty contact configured for the site receives CRITICAL messages with approve and dismiss links. Every decision is logged with its time and source.

Is anyone's privacy affected?

Cameras look at ground and structures. There is no person detection or face processing anywhere in the code, and raw images expire after 30 days.

Glossary

Epoch
One burst of frames at one time.
Subset
A small image patch matched between epochs.
ZNCC
Zero-normalised cross-correlation, a match score immune to brightness changes.
Coverage
Share of subsets that passed quality checks.
Onset of acceleration
First epoch of sustained speed-up.
Inverse velocity
One divided by velocity; falls linearly to zero at failure for creep.
CAMERA_EVENT
The stable ground appeared to move; the camera, not the slope, is suspect.
BLIND / BLURRED
Too dark, foggy, or wet to measure; reported, not hidden.
